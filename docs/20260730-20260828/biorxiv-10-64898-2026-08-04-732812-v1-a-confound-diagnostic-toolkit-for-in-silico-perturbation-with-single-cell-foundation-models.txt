Summary 14 

Deleting a gene token from a cell’s input sequence offers a convenient native strategy for in 15 

silico perturbation, but the resulting embedding delta may not represent a biological knockout 16 

response. Apparent effects can instead reflect gene identity, universal responsiveness, limited 17 

tokenization coverage, library -size contamination, or circular state scoring. Here, we present a 18 

confound -diagnostic framework combining held -out increment testing, responsiveness 19 

adjustment, coverage gating, library -size diagnostics, and de -circularized state -shift analysis, 20 

together with a numerically matched reimplementation of frozen  Geneformer’s perturbation 21 

engine. Across Frangieh and Replogle datasets and linear and nonlinear readouts, the native 22 

embedding delta provided no reproducible held -out improvement beyond gene identity. Signal -23 

injection calibration showed that the test  detected injected  residual signal, whereas native 24 

increments remained below its detection floor. Matched controls traced apparent positives to 25 

raw -count  library -size  structure, broad responsiveness, and self -referential scoring, while 26 

coverage constrained perturbation applicability and estimate stability without establishing 27 

biological specificity. This model -adaptable framework helps determine when foundation -model 28 

perturbation readouts warrant biological interpretation. 29

Motivation 31 

Foundation -model in silico perturbation could  predict perturbation effects  when matched 32 

experimental data are unavailable. However, in zero -shot settings, embedding -derived responses 33 

may reflect gene identity, universal responsiveness, tokenization limits, library -size artifacts, or 34 

circular state scoring rather than biological knockout effects. We therefore developed a reusable 35 

confound -diagnostic framework that applies matched controls to test whether native perturbation 36 

readouts contain information beyond these confounds and warrant biological interpretation. 37

Introduction 38 

Single -cell foundation models, including Geneformer 1, scGPT 2 and scFoundation 3, have created 39 

new opportunities for learning transferable representations of genes and cells from large -scale 40 

transcriptomic data. One proposed application is zero -shot in silico perturbation. In tokenized 41 

models such as Geneformer , this can be implemented by removing a target -gene token from a 42 

cell’s input sequence and interpreting the resulting change in model embedding, referred to here 43 

as the embedding delta, as a predicted knockout response. This native token -deletion strategy is 44 

attractive because it could, in principle, estimate perturbation effects when matched experimental 45 

perturbation data are unavailable. It also differs from network -inference and supervised 46 

perturbation -response methods, such as CellOracle 4, scGen 5, and GEARS 6, which typically 47 

require additional regulatory information, chromatin data, or perturbation training datasets. 48 

Despite this promise, the biological validity of native foundation -model perturbation readouts 49 

remains unresolved. Recent benchmarking studies have reported limited zero -shot  utility on 50 

general single -cell tasks 7 and on perturbation -specific evaluations 8,9 , modest or inconsistent gains 51 

over simple baselines 10,11 , and substantial sensitivity to metric choice, normalization strategy, 52 

baseline definition, and target readout 12 . These findings indicate that apparent predictive 53 

performance alone is insufficient to establish perturbation -specific biological signal. Rigorous 54 

evaluation therefore requires determining whether an embedding -derived perturbation readout 55 

provides information beyond confounding structure in the model representation, input data, and 56 

evaluation design. 57 

Several confounding factors can inflate apparent perturbation readouts, making them appear 58 

informative without necessarily reflecting perturbation -specific biological signal 13 . Gene identity 59 

and baseline expression structure can make affected genes predictable even in the absence of a 60

Results 91 

A numerically matched reimplementation enables confound -controlled testing of 92 

embedding -delta signal 93 

We first established a reproducible reimplementation of Geneformer’s native in silico knockout 94 

response. In this token -deletion procedure, the target -gene token is removed from each cell’s 95 

input sequence, and the resulting change in gene or cell embeddings is summarized as the native 96 

perturbation response. We reimplemented the per -cell gene cosine -similarity response to target -97 

gene deletion, referred to as path -D, and validated it against the official Geneformer 98 

InSilicoPerturber  output. Path -D reproduced the official output to numerical precision, with 99 

Pearson’s r>0.999999 and a maximum absolute error of 3×10 −7.100 

This matched reimplementation provided the computational basis for the confound -diagnostic 101 

workflow. Cached per -cell path -D responses enabled controlled resampling while holding the 102 

model checkpoint, tokenization, target gene, and perturbation operator fixed. The workflow 103 

incorporated held -out increment testing against a gene -identity baseline, universal -104 

responsiveness adjustment, tokenization -coverage gating, library -size diagnostics, and de -105 

circularized cell -state -shift analysis ( Figure 1 ; Table 1 ). 106 

We first asked whether the native embedding delta improved prediction of experimentally 107 

measured perturbation effects beyond gene identity. In the Frangieh Perturb -CITE -seq dataset, 108 

we evaluated CCND1 knockout using a DESeq2 -derived pseudobulk differential -expression 109 

target. The unperturbed Geneformer embedding of each evaluated gene served as the baseline, 110 

and the matched baseline -plus -delta model additionally included the 768 -dimensional knockout 111 

embedding delta. 112

The gene -identity baseline achieved a held -out Pearson correlation of r=0.487. Adding the 113 

embedding delta yielded an increment of Δr=−0.004. Similar near -zero increments were 114 

observed for ridge regression, a regularization -swept multilayer perceptron, and gradient -boosted 115 

trees (Δr=−0.004, −0.024, and +0.010, respectively;  Figure 2A ). Thus, across linear and 116 

nonlinear readout models, the native embedding delta did not provide a reproducible held -out 117 

improvement beyond the matched gene -identity baseline. 118 

Embedding -delta increments remain near zero across datasets and perturbation programs 119 

We next examined whether this result generalized to a more heterogeneous perturbation dataset. 120 

Among perturbations represented by at least 50 cells, the mean pairwise perturbation -profile 121 

correlation was +0.06 in the Replogle K562 CRISPRi  dataset, compared with +0.40 in the 122 

Frangieh dataset, indicating greater diversity among Replogle transcriptional responses. Split -123 

half reliability for the selected Replogle perturbations ranged from 0.88 to 0.99, confirming that 124 

these perturbation programs were reproducible. 125 

Despite this heterogeneity, embedding -delta increments on DESeq2 -derived targets remained 126 

near zero across perturbations representing distinct biological processes ( Figure 2B ). The 127 

increments were −0.016 for HSPA9, −0.004 for SUPT6H, −0.014 for CSE1L, and −0.008 for 128 

DHX15. Each perturbation was supported by 35 –46 pseudobulk replicates, compared with 48 129 

controls, and showed strong agreement between DESeq2 and size -factor -normalized fold -change 130 

directions. 131

GATA1 was included as an additional, biologically informative case. Although GATA1 132 

knockdown produced the strongest transcriptional program among the evaluated perturbations, 133 

with 766 differentially expressed genes, its embedding -delta increment was also near zero 134 

(Δr=−0.004). Because only three pseudobulk replicates were available and the sign -check value 135 

was +0.66, GATA1 was considered supportive rather than primary evidence. 136 

These findings argue against dataset homogeneity or weak experimental perturbation effects as 137 

explanations for the near -zero increments. The Replogle dataset contained diverse and 138 

reproducible perturbation -specific transcriptional programs, yet the native Geneformer 139 

embedding delta did not recover detectable information beyond gene identity. 140 

Finally, we calibrated the sensitivity of the held -out increment test by introducing features with 141 

known residual signal beyond the gene -identity baseline. A detectable increment emerged when 142 

the injected feature had a per -gene correlation of approximately c ∗=0.20 with the residual target. 143 

By contrast, the native delta increment remained within the prespecified near -zero band 144 

(Δr=−0.004;  Figure 2C ). Under this calibration, any perturbation -specific information carried by 145 

the native embedding delta was therefore below the detection threshold of the analysis. 146

As a complementary positive control, we confirmed that the increment test recovers genuine 147 

perturbation signal, not only synthetic injections. A real, held -out measurement of the CCND1 148 

effect —computed on a disjoint half of the CCND1 cells and passed through the identical 149 

machinery —produced a clear positive increment (Δr  = +0.092 ± 0.016), whereas the native 150 

embedding delta and a random -noise feature both remained within the near -zero band (Δr = 151 

−0.005 and −0.000, respectively;  Figure 2D ). Thus the native delta behaves like the noise 152 

control rather than like a real perturbation feature, indicating that the near -zero native increment 153 

reflects an absence of signal rather than an insensitive test. 154 

Matched controls identify confounds underlying apparently informative readouts 155 

We next examined exploratory readouts that initially appeared informative but were not intended 156 

as primary evidence of perturbation -specific signal. These analyses served as diagnostic stress 157 

tests to determine whether the observed signals persisted after matched confound controls. 158 

Directional and scalar readouts were sensitive to score construction and target definition. An 159 

oracle -direction diagnostic yielded a correlation of r=+0.39, but the scoring direction was 160 

defined using the target itself; reversing that direction reversed the sign of the correlation by 161 

construction. A simple readout based on response magnitude and projection achieved r=+0.27 162 

against a raw -count log2 fold -change target, but only r=+0.01 against the primary DESeq2 -163 

derived target. Thus, the apparent performance was largely dependent on raw -count structure 164 

rather than a robust perturbation -specific signal. 165

Affected -gene saliency also reflected broad responsiveness. The initial saliency readout 171 

correlated with the CCND1 effect at r=−0.34, but the partial correlation after adjustment for 172

Together, these matched controls substantially attenuated the apparently informative exploratory 176 

readouts. Directional performance depended on target -defined orientation, raw -count 177 

performance disappeared after normalized differential -expression analysis, high -dimensional 178 

probes did not improve over gene identity, and saliency was removed by adjustment for 179 

universal responsiveness ( Figure 3B ). 180 

Universal responsiveness and library -size structure drive exploratory signal inflation 181 

Across 232 Frangieh perturbations, each gene’s universal mean absolute response was strongly 182 

associated with the absolute CCND1 effect (Spearman’s ρ=+0.868). In contrast, embedding -shift 183 

magnitude was inversely associated with biological effect magnitude (ρ=−0.45;  Figure 3C ). 184 

These findings indicate that apparent affected -gene saliency was dominated by broadly 185 

responsive genes and that larger embedding shifts did not preferentially correspond to larger 186 

biological effects. 187

De -circularization does not support pathway -specific cell -state movement 198 

We also evaluated whether native Geneformer  knockout shifted cell embeddings toward 199 

experimentally observed perturbation states. In the Replogle K562 dataset, in silico GATA1 200 

knockout initially appeared to move cells toward the experimental GATA1 -knockdown state, 201 

with z≈+4.6 relative to a small random -gene null (n=10). 202 

To reduce self -referential scoring, we constructed a cross -gene projection matrix in which the in 203 

silico  knockout of gene A was projected onto the experimental perturbation -state axis of a 204 

different gene B. This design separated the deleted gene from the axis used for evaluation. 205 

Deterministic comparisons among experimentally perturbed genes served as the primary analysis, 206 

while the seed -fixed random -gene null was used as a sensitivity check. 207 

After de -circularization, the projection matrix showed no  evidence of the expected  pathway -208 

specific structure. If native knockout induced pathway -directed state movement, same -pathway 209

Tokenization coverage constrains perturbation applicability and estimate stability 218 

We next examined tokenization coverage, defined as the fraction of cells in which the target -219 

gene token was present in the top -4096 input sequence. When the target token was absent, no 220 

deletion occurred and the cell did not contribute to the native knockout estimate. Across 217 in -221 

vocabulary Frangieh targets, median coverage was 47%. Several biologically relevant targets had 222 

substantially lower coverage, including JAK2 at 2.3% and CD274 at 11% ( Figure 4A ), 223 

indicating that native token deletion was applicable to only a small fraction of cells for some 224 

perturbations. 225 

The numerically matched path -D implementation enabled direct evaluation of coverage effects. 226 

We repeatedly subsampled token -containing cells while holding the model checkpoint, 227 

tokenization, target gene, and perturbation operator fixed, thereby isolating the effect of the 228 

number of contributing cells. 229 

For the exploratory raw -count CCND1 target, the recovered knockout estimate changed 230 

systematically with the number of contributing cells and converged as this number increased 231

Discussion 241 

This study presents a reusable confound -diagnostic workflow for evaluating native foundation -242 

model in silico perturbation readouts. Using frozen Geneformer  token deletion as a case study, 243 

we found that the native embedding delta provided no reproducible held -out improvement 244 

beyond a gene -identity baseline across two perturbation datasets and linear and nonlinear readout 245 

models. Signal -injection calibration confirmed that the increment test could recover added 246 

residual signal, whereas the native delta remained below the detection threshold of the analysis. 247 

Matched diagnostics further showed that apparently informative readouts could arise from gene 248 

identity, universal responsiveness, library -size structure, and self -referential state scoring. 249 

Tokenization coverage imposed an additional constraint on perturbation applicability and 250 

estimate stability but did not establish biological specificity. 251 

The primary evidence came from held -out increment testing. Adding the 768 -dimensional 252 

knockout embedding delta to unperturbed gene embeddings did not improve prediction of 253 

experimentally measured perturbation effects. This result was consistent across ridge regression, 254 

multilayer perceptrons, and gradient -boosted trees and across the Frangieh Perturb -CITE -seq and 255 

Replogle K562 CRISPRi  datasets. Importantly, the Replogle dataset contained heterogeneous 256 

and reproducible perturbation -specific transcriptional programs, arguing against weak or 257 

homogeneous benchmark signal as the explanation for the near -zero increments. Moreover, the 258 

signal -injection analysis recovered detectable increments once the injected feature carried 259 

sufficient residual information, whereas the native delta remained within the near -zero band. 260 

Thus, under the tested design, any perturbation -specific information carried by the native 261 

embedding delta was below the detection threshold rather than obscured by an insensitive 262 

evaluation. 263

The exploratory analyses identified several distinct mechanisms of apparent signal inflation. 264 

Unperturbed gene embeddings supported substantial predictability without information from the 265 

knockout operation, indicating that gene identity and baseline expression structure can be 266 

mistaken for perturbation -specific signal. Affected -gene saliency largely reflected genes that 267 

responded broadly across many perturbations rather than specifically to CCND1 knockout. Raw -268 

count fold -change targets contained library -size structure that inflated apparent predictability and 269 

cross -perturbation correlations. Cell -state -shift readouts became misleading when the deleted 270 

gene also contributed to the experimental axis used for scoring; after cross -gene de -271 

circularization, the expected pathway -specific structure was not observed. These findings 272 

emphasize that apparently strong correlations are insufficient unless the corresponding 273 

alternative explanations are explicitly controlled. 274 

Tokenization coverage represents a related but conceptually distinct limitation. Native deletion 275 

can occur only in cells whose truncated input sequence contains the target token. Across 276 

Frangieh targets, coverage varied widely, and several biologically relevant genes were 277 

represented in only a small fraction of cells. Controlled subsampling showed that the recovered 278 

estimate changed systematically with the number of contributing cells and converged as that 279 

number increased. However, convergence stabilized the evaluated readout without demonstrating 280 

that it was biologically specific. Coverage should therefore be treated as an applicability and 281 

stability criterion rather than as evidence supporting biological validity. 282 

The contribution of this study is not an additional aggregate benchmark of perturbation 283 

prediction, but a framework for determining why a native perturbation readout appears 284 

informative and whether that signal persists after matched controls. The numerically matched 285 

path -D implementation enables controlled manipulation of the number of contributing cells 286

identity, whereas universal -responsiveness adjustment, library -size diagnostics, and de -289

tokenization coverage is specific to token -based models, other architectures will require 291 

analogous applicability checks to verify that the perturbation operator meaningfully alters the 292 

relevant model input or representation. 293 

The empirical scope of the study remains intentionally limited. We evaluated frozen Geneformer 294 

native token deletion and did not test fine -tuned perturbation models, decoder -based 295 

architectures, inference -time steering, or alternative perturbation operators. The findings 296 

therefore should not be generalized to all single -cell foundation models or perturbation -modeling 297 

strategies. The cell -state analysis used K562 perturbation axes rather than an independent 298 

differentiation or functional -state reference, and the GATA1 analysis had limited pseudobulk 299 

replication. In addition, the coverage -to -estimate analysis assessed estimate stability using an 300 

exploratory readout and did not establish biological accuracy. These limitations define the scope 301 

of the conclusions but do not change the central result: under the tested conditions, the native 302 

Geneformer embedding delta provided no detectable perturbation -specific information beyond 303 

gene identity. 304 

Native token deletion remains computationally simple and potentially useful as a hypothesis -305 

generating operation, but deletion of an input token should not be assumed to reproduce 306 

biological loss of function. Future methods may improve perturbation modeling through 307 

supervised fine -tuning, decoder -based response prediction, inference -time steering 14 , rank -aware 308 

evaluation 15 , or perturbation operators designed to better approximate biological intervention. 309

Geneformer model and native in silico knockout 431 

All analyses were performed using the frozen  Geneformer V2 -104M model without fine -tuning. 432 

The model contains 12 transformer layers, a hidden dimension of 768, and a gene -token 433 

vocabulary of approximately 20,271 tokens. The specific model checkpoint, Geneformer version, 434 

and commit identifier are listed in the Key resources table. Unless otherwise specified, 435 

embeddings were extracted from the second -to -last hidden layer (hidden_states[11]; emb_layer = 436 

−1 in Geneformer's convention). 437

Native in silico knockout was performed by deleting the target -gene token from each cell’s rank -438 

ordered top -4096 token sequence. Wild -type and knockout forward passes were then compared 439 

to obtain gene -level or cell -level embedding responses. Gene embeddings were extracted for 440 

evaluated genes, whereas cell embeddings were represented by the classification token. Cells in 441 

which the target -gene token was absent were not assigned a zero response, because no token 442 

deletion occurred in those cells. These cells were excluded from the native knockout estimate. 443 

Tokenization coverage 444 

Tokenization coverage was defined as the fraction of sampled input cells in which the target -445 

gene token was present in the top -4096 token sequence. The denominator was the total number 446 

of sampled cells, and the numerator was the number of cells containing the target token. This 447 

definition separates biological cell sampling from perturbation applicability in the tokenized 448 

model input. Coverage values were recorded for each target and used to determine whether 449 

native token deletion could be meaningfully applied in the sampled cell population. 450 

Path -D implementation and validation 451 

We implemented the per -cell gene cosine -similarity response to target -gene deletion directly 452 

from the frozen Geneformer model and refer to this response as path -D. Validation was 453 

performed by comparing path -D outputs with the official Geneformer InSilicoPerturber outputs 454 

on the same input cells and target genes. Pearson correlation and maximum absolute error were 455 

used as numerical validation metrics. The validation results are reported in the Results. 456 

After validation, per -cell path -D responses were cached and used for downstream NumPy -based 457 

resampling analyses. This implementation allowed repeated subsampling of contributing cells 458 

without rerunning the transformer model. In the coverage -to -estimate analysis, the number of 459

Experimental reference differential expression 463 

Primary log2 fold -change targets were computed by pseudobulk aggregation followed by 464 

DESeq2 19  with median -of -ratios size factors, implemented in PyDESeq2 20 ; edgeR 21  provides an 465 

equivalent count -based alternative . The exact PyDESeq2 version, DESeq2 -compatible settings, 466 

and preprocessing scripts are listed in the Key resources table and code repository. 467 

Pseudobulk replicates were defined separately for each dataset to avoid cell -level 468 

pseudoreplication 22 . In the Frangieh dataset, pseudobulk replicates were defined by single -guide 469 

RNA. In the Replogle dataset, pseudobulk replicates were defined by gemgroup batch. The non -470 

targeting or control population served as the reference group. Log fold changes were estimated 471 

with apeglm shrinkage 23 . For each perturbation target, a sign check was performed to confirm 472 

that the DESeq2 log fold change agreed in direction with the corresponding size -factor -473 

normalized log2 fold change. 474 

Raw -count log2 fold change was not used as a primary evaluation target. It was used only as an 475 

exploratory diagnostic to assess library -size contamination. 476 

Held -out increment testing 477 

For each readout, we evaluated whether the native knockout embedding delta added predictive 478 

information beyond a matched baseline feature set. The primary baseline was the wild -type gene 479 

embedding, defined as the unperturbed embedding of each evaluated gene. This baseline 480

For each model class, the baseline feature set was compared with the same feature set plus the 483 

768 -dimensional embedding delta. Both feature sets were evaluated using the same model class 484 

and the same cross -validation splits. The evaluated readout models included ridge regression, a 485 

regularization -swept multilayer perceptron, and gradient -boosted trees. All readout models used 486 

a fixed random seed (0) and 5 -fold cross -validation (KFold, shuffle enabled, random_state  = 0), 487 

with feature standardization fit on the training fold only to avoid leakage. Ridge regression used 488 

RidgeCV over 13 logarithmically spaced L2 penalties (10⁻² to 10⁴). The multilayer perceptron 489 

used a single hidden layer of 32 units with early stopping (max_iter = 400, n_iter_no_change = 490 

10), and its L2 penalty (α) was swept over {1, 10, 30, 100}. Gradient -boosted trees used a 491 

histogram -based gradient -boosting regressor (max_depth  = 3, L2 regularization = 1.0, early 492 

stopping). Full configurations and seeds are provided in the deposited analysis code 493 

(scripts/diagnostics/nonlinear_seal.py). 494

were not used as evidence of predictive performance. Delta increment was defined as the 497

Universal responsiveness was used to assess whether affected -gene saliency reflected 513 

perturbation -specific response or broad responsiveness across perturbations. For each evaluated 514 

gene, a universal -responsiveness score was computed from its mean absolute response across 515 

perturbations satisfying the relevant inclusion criteria. Saliency analyses then evaluated whether 516 

apparent  perturbation -specific signal remained after conditioning on or subtracting this universal -517

De -circularized cell -state -shift analysis 525 

Cell -state -shift analyses evaluated whether an in silico  knockout moved cells toward an 526 

experimentally observed perturbation state in embedding space. To reduce self -referential 527 

scoring, we constructed a de -circularization matrix in which the knockout of gene A was 528 

projected onto the perturbation -state axis of a different gene B. This cross -gene projection design 529 

separates the deleted gene from the state axis used for scoring. 530 

Same -pathway off -diagonal projections were compared with cross -pathway projections to 531 

evaluate pathway -specific state movement after separating the deleted gene from the scoring axis. 532 

A seed -fixed random -gene null was used as a sensitivity check. Gene groups, projection axes, 533 

and random -null genes are provided in  Supplementary Table S1 .534 

Coverage -to -estimate analysis 535 

Coverage -to -estimate analysis was performed using cached path -D responses. For a fixed target 536 

gene and perturbation operator, contributing cells were repeatedly subsampled from the token -537 

containing cell set. The recovered native -knockout estimate was recomputed after each 538 

subsampling step. This procedure manipulated the number of contributing cells while holding the 539 

model, per -cell tokenization, target gene, and perturbation operator fixed. Fixed -direction and 540 

faithfully resampled estimates were compared to identify which component of the estimate was 541 

most sensitive to coverage. 542
