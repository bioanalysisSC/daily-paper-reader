Gene regulatory network (GRN) inference from single ‐cell RNA ‐seq 26 

conventionally relies on per ‐dataset optimization. Existing tools must be refit for 27 

every new dataset, and the majority fail to infer causal regulatory directions. 28 

Here we present BoYueGRN, an amortized causal discovery framework trained 29 

exclusively on 10,000 synthetic structural causal models. For any unseen 30 

dataset, a single forward pass returns edge probabilities and regulatory 31 

directions, while TF-centric sliding windows with asymmetric fusion extend this 32 

fixed-size model to full-transcriptome coverage. BoYueGRN demonstrates 33 

strong zero ‐shot performance across BEELINE benchmarks. On two 34 

independent genome-wide CRISPRi Perturb-seq screens, directional accuracy 35 

on retained edges reaches 0.86 and 0.95. Reconstructed cell-type- and stage-36 

specific GRN dynamics across five diseases spanning more than 270,000 cells 37 

yield experimentally testable biological hypotheses. BoYueGRN reframes 38 

directed GRN inference as a train-once, reuse-across-datasets paradigm. By 39 

decoupling network reconstruction from per-dataset optimization, this paradigm 40 

opens the door to systematic, atlas-scale mapping of regulatory dynamics 41 

across human diseases. 42 

Introduction 43 

Cellular states and functions are orchestrated by GRN, in which transcription 44 

factors activate or repress target genes along directed edges[1]. Dissecting 45 

these directed relationships is therefore a prerequisite for mechanistic 46 

interpretation of cell identity, differentiation, and disease. Single-cell RNA 47 

sequencing now provides transcriptomic measurements at single-cell 48 

resolution and at a scale of millions of cells, raising the prospect of 49 

reconstructing GRNs directly from steady-state single-cell data[2, 3] 50 

A wide range of methods has been proposed to infer GRNs from single-cell 51 

transcriptomes, yet systematic evaluations indicate that expression-based 52 

reconstruction remains only partially reliable. Early approaches such as 53 

ARACNE, CLR, and PIDC score pairwise associations using mutual 54 

information or multivariate information measures adapted to single-cell 55 

sparsity[4-6]. GENIE3 and its scalable variant GRNBoost2 regress each gene 56 

on all remaining genes[7, 8], whereas Gaussian graphical models and 57

recent deep-learning models infer regulatory interactions directly from 62 

expression profiles[16-19], and pretrained foundation models such as 63 

Geneformer, scGPT, and RegFormer offer transferable single-cell 64 

representations[20-22]; expression-driven transformer GRN methods such as 65 

STGRNS and GeneCompass likewise remain per-dataset or per-corpus 66 

learners rather than amortized, train-once directed inference as pursued here. 67 

Nevertheless, community assessments of bulk expression profiles have 68 

concluded that reliable network inference from gene expression remains an 69 

unsolved problem[23, 24], and on the scRNA-seq-oriented BEELINE 70 

benchmark most algorithms perform only marginally above random on real 71 

networks[25]. 72 

Beyond accuracy, three structural limitations constrain the utility of existing 73 

tools. The first is coverage: methods whose computational cost grows 74 

quadratically with gene number are typically confined to a few hundred highly 75 

variable genes, excluding the majority of low- and mid-expression disease-76 

relevant genes. The second is directionality: most tools emit only undirected 77 

associations, and prior-knowledge methods can orient edges only where ChIP-78 

seq or motif evidence exists, which is incomplete for most cell types and TFs. 79 

The third is the fitting strategy: current methods re-optimize parameters 80 

separately for each dataset, which is slow and unstable when genes vastly 81 

outnumber cells, and they are designed for a single snapshot rather than for 82 

disease progression resolved across stages and cell types. A method that 83 

simultaneously delivers genome-wide coverage, regulatory direction, and train-84 

once-reuse-everywhere efficiency remains an unmet need. 85 

Amortized causal discovery offers precisely such a strategy by replacing per-86 

dataset optimization with a model trained once on simulated data and then 87 

applied to new data in a single forward pass. Identifiability theory establishes 88 

that causal direction can be recovered from observational data alone in both 89 

linear non-Gaussian and nonlinear additive noise models[26, 27], and structure 90

methodological gap for scalable, directed GRN inference from observational 106 

single-cell data. 107 

Here we present BoYueGRN, an amortized causal discovery framework for 108 

directed GRN inference from scRNA-seq that closes this gap. The model is 109 

trained entirely on 10,000 synthetic expression datasets generated by structural 110 

causal models (SCMs) whose topology, noise mechanisms, and expression 111 

statistics are aligned with real GRNs; for any new dataset, a single forward pass 112 

returns edge probabilities and regulatory directions, and TF-centric sliding 113 

windows extend this fixed-size model to full-transcriptome coverage. The name 114 

BoYue, drawn from the Chinese adage “ bo guan er yue qu” (coined by the 115 

renowned Song dynasty poet Su Shi, meaning "survey broadly, then extract 116 

selectively"), captures this design: broad pretraining on synthetic causal models 117 

followed by selective inference in a single forward pass. Having seen no real 118 

data during training, BoYueGRN already outperforms several mainstream 119 

methods at the TF-centric single-window scale and achieves the best AUROC 120 

on all six BEELINE datasets under full-genome coverage; on two genome-wide 121 

CRISPRi perturbation screens it attains strong directional accuracy. Building on 122 

these benchmarks, we apply BoYueGRN to five disease settings spanning 123

The BoYueGRN framework 132 

BoYueGRN is an amortized framework for directed GRN inference from 133 

scRNA-seq, trained exclusively on synthetic SCMs and, once trained, requiring 134 

only a single forward pass to produce a directed, genome-wide GRN for any 135 

new real dataset (Fig. 1). 136 

We first construct the training corpus by simulating 10,000 synthetic expression 137 

datasets whose graph topology is generated by preferential attachment and 138 

whose expression follows additive noise structural equations (Fig. 1a, b). A five-139 

node seed causal chain is created first, after which each node selects parents 140 

from earlier-indexed nodes with probability proportional to in-degree plus one; 141 

constraining parents to earlier-indexed nodes guarantees acyclicity by 142 

construction and reproduces the scale-free hub structure of real GRNs[34]. 143

Edge and direction prediction benchmarks 227 

We next test whether the edge-prediction and direction-inference abilities 228 

established on synthetic data transfer to real scRNA-seq. Zero-shot evaluation 229 

is performed on the six BEELINE benchmark datasets (mDC, mHSC-E, mHSC-230 

GM, mHSC-L, hESC, hHep), with ground-truth networks taken from the ChIP-231 

seq-derived networks released with BEELINE, and BoYueGRN is compared 232 

against seven established methods (GENIE3, GRNBoost2, Correlation, 233 

MutualInfo, PPCOR, GLasso, and dCor) plus a random baseline on identical 234 

inputs. Direction prediction is assessed on the same benchmark and further 235 

validated against causal ground truth on two genome-wide Perturb-seq screens. 236 

Edge-existence prediction. At the TF-centric single-window setting (G=200), 237 

BoYueGRN attains the highest AUROC on five of six datasets (mHSC-E 0.749, 238 

mHSC-GM 0.760, mHSC-L 0.751, hESC 0.736, hHep 0.604; mean 0.679; Fig. 239 

3a). Beyond overall ranking, the early precision ratio (EPR), the enrichment 240 

multiple of ground-truth edges among the top-k predictions, exceeds 2 on four 241 

of six datasets, peaking at 2.97 for mHSC-L (Fig. 3b). Extending the model to 242 

full-genome coverage via window sliding raises the mean AUROC to 0.760 and 243 

keeps EPR above 4 on all six datasets (Fig. 3c–d). 244

Rank-deficiency guard for Pearson preselection at low cell-to-gene ratios. 296 

Datasets with a low cell-to-gene ratio require a targeted guard to keep 297 

correlation-based preselection statistically stable. When the ratio of sampled 298 

cells to genes is low, the Pearson correlation matrix tends to be rank-deficient 299 

and correlation-based target preselection degenerates into noise-driven 300 

ranking, so the candidate gene set must be shrunk by variance. We 301 

encountered this with the BEELINE hESC dataset (758 cells, 17,735 genes): 302 

its subsampled cell-to-gene ratio of 500/17,735 = 0.028 falls below the 303 

preselection stability threshold of 0.04, the Pearson correlation matrix is 304 

severely rank-deficient (rank ≤ 500), per-TF top-50 target selection is 305 

dominated by noise and effectively random, within-window ground-truth edge 306 

coverage collapses, and the genome-wide AUROC is only 0.501 (random level) 307 

with an EPR of 1.99×—clearly below the other five datasets (EPR 4.64–5.31). 308 

We therefore introduce adaptive variance-based subsampling: when the ratio 309 

falls below the threshold, candidates are restricted to the n_keep genes of 310

Expanded gene selection recovers disease-relevant pathways missed by 348 

conventional selection 349 

Because the scale of the input gene set directly constrains biological discovery, 350 

we developed a DEG-Expanded strategy that replaces conventional HVG 351 

selection and validated it on a multi-stage non-alcoholic fatty liver disease 352 

(NAFLD; renamed metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease, 353 

MASLD, by the 2023 Delphi consensus, with NASH correspondingly MASH) 354 

dataset[39]. BoYueGRN trains and infers on fixed-size (G=200) gene subsets 355 

and achieves genome-wide coverage through sliding windows, but 356 

conventional highly variable gene (HVG) selection typically retains only a few 357 

hundred genes (for example 350), a cap that can constrain downstream 358 

biological mining. To break this constraint, the DEG-Expanded strategy 359

validate it on a NAFLD cohort (GSE202379, 99,809 cells × 30,908 genes; five 361 

stages: Healthy, NAFLD, NASH, Cirrhosis, End-stage). NAFLD is the most 362 

common chronic liver disease worldwide and can progress from simple 363 

steatosis through steatohepatitis (NASH) to cirrhosis and hepatocellular 364 

carcinoma. To isolate the effect of gene coverage on discovery, we merge all 365 

cell types, run GO enrichment[40, 41] on the expanded input gene set, and 366

Discussion 505 

BoYueGRN reframes directed GRN inference as a train-once, cross-dataset 506 

inference paradigm, replacing the per-dataset optimization that has long 507 

constrained the field. Trained entirely on synthetic structural causal models, the 508 

model reconstructs genome-scale directed regulatory networks from real 509 

scRNA-seq data in a single forward pass, simultaneously delivering the 510 

prediction accuracy, gene coverage, and reliability of regulatory direction that 511 

existing tools struggle to combine. We validated its performance on the 512 

BEELINE benchmark and two Perturb-seq screens, and, applying the same 513 

pipeline to five disease datasets, the model not only reproduced reported 514 

pathological features but also generated experimentally testable regulatory 515 

hypotheses, demonstrating both reliable predictive ability and mechanistic 516 

discovery value. 517 

To address the high ‐dimensionality, noise, and directional constraints inherent 518 

to scRNA ‐seq GRN inference, we adopt an amortized causal discovery design. 519 

GENIE3, GRNBoost2, graphical lasso, and NOTEARS all refit models per 520 

dataset, which is slow and unstable when p ≫n; an amortized model trains once 521 

and then infers at near-zero marginal cost. This lineage extends from the time-522 

series-oriented ACD and the i.i.d.-setting AVICI to the CDFM foundation 523 

model[29, 30, 32], yet none targets GRN inference, and even the closest 524 

predecessor, AVICI, has been validated only on synthetic and semi-synthetic 525 

data. What enables the transfer is the alignment of the synthetic training 526 

distribution with real GRNs along three axes: (1) scale-free hub topology from 527 

preferential attachment; (2) an additive-noise mechanism that separates 528 

injection noise from final noise to render directions identifiable; and (3) log1p-529 

transformed, sparse, non-negative expression statistics. Whereas simulators 530

BoYueGRN instead uses synthetic causal models directly as training material 532

Applying the framework to GRN-dynamics reconstruction across five diseases 549 

spanning neoplastic, precancerous, and neurodegenerative settings recovered 550 

pathology-coherent regulatory programs and generated testable hypotheses in 551 

every case, with most of these signals recovered only at cell-type resolution 552

prior-free direction inference, genome-wide coverage, and disease-network 615

8. Benchmark evaluation 778 

BEELINE zero-shot benchmark. The six datasets are compared against the 779 

same seven methods plus a random baseline on identical inputs. BEELINE 780 

additionally provides a seventh dataset (mESC), which we exclude because it 781 

is the only time-course entry in the benchmark: its 421 cells pool five 782 

differentiation time points (0-72 h), violating the steady-state, exchangeable-783 

cells assumption on which the input statistics P and D rest, whereas the other 784 

six datasets are single-condition snapshots. The window-level benchmark uses 785 

a G=200 single window (top-50 TFs + the 150 target genes of highest 786 

expression variance, padded with the highest-variance genes when fewer than 787 

200), ensuring fair comparison with baselines; the genome-wide benchmark 788 

covers all genes with the Section 7 sliding windows. Metrics are AUROC, EPR, 789 

ECR, and DirAcc (defined in Section 11). Perturb-seq direction validation. 790 

Because ChIP-seq ground truth reflects physical binding rather than causal 791 

regulation, direction inference is further validated on two human Perturb-seq 792 

datasets (HCT116 and K562; ground-truth construction in Section 2). 793 

Directional accuracy is computed only on predicted edges (edge_prob > 0.2); 794 

shuffled controls (randomly permuted ground-truth directions) test for 795 

systematic bias in direction judgment. Confidence calibration. Predicted 796 

edges are binned by edge_prob into low/mid/high bins (bin center 797 

0.10/0.50/0.90), and the empirical DirAcc per bin is computed to test 798 

consistency between model probability and independently validated accuracy; 799 

an optional high-confidence filter (edge_prob ≥ 0.65) can further restrict edges 800 

when stricter precision is desired, although the analyses reported here operate 801 

on the τ=0.2 emission threshold (Section 7). 802
